# Task 1 – Product Vision and Scrum Roles

## Project: Online Grocery Delivery Application

### 1. Product Vision Statement

To develop a user-friendly and reliable online grocery delivery application that enables customers to browse products, search for groceries, manage shopping carts, place orders, make secure payments, and track deliveries from their homes.

The application aims to simplify grocery shopping by providing a convenient digital experience, accurate product availability, flexible delivery options, secure transactions, and timely order updates.

**Target Users:**
- Working professionals
- Families and households
- Elderly customers
- Individuals who prefer online grocery shopping

**Business Goals:**
- Improve customer convenience and satisfaction.
- Increase online sales and customer engagement.
- Reduce manual order-processing activities.
- Improve delivery efficiency.
- Build long-term customer loyalty.

### 2. Scrum Roles and Responsibilities

Scrum defines three accountabilities within a Scrum Team: Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers.

#### A. Product Owner

**Responsibilities:**
- Establish and communicate the product goal.
- Manage and prioritise the product backlog.
- Ensure user stories deliver business value.
- Understand customer requirements.
- Collaborate with stakeholders and clarify product expectations.

**Anti-pattern:**
The Product Owner allows multiple stakeholders to independently assign work to Developers without maintaining a single prioritised product backlog.

**Why it is an anti-pattern:**
It creates confusion, conflicting priorities, and a lack of clear direction for the development team.

#### B. Scrum Master

**Responsibilities:**
- Help establish Scrum practices within the team.
- Facilitate effective Scrum events.
- Identify and help remove impediments.
- Coach team members in Agile principles.
- Support collaboration between the Product Owner, Developers, and stakeholders.

**Anti-pattern:**
The Scrum Master behaves like a traditional project manager by assigning tasks to Developers and controlling their daily work.

**Why it is an anti-pattern:**
Scrum encourages self-managing teams. The Scrum Master should support the team rather than control individual task assignments.

#### C. Developers

**Responsibilities:**
- Design, develop, test, and deliver a usable product increment.
- Plan and manage their work during each Sprint.
- Collaborate to achieve the Sprint Goal.
- Maintain product quality by following the Definition of Done.
- Adapt their daily work based on progress and changing requirements.

**Anti-pattern:**
Developers work in isolated silos, with programmers, testers, and designers refusing to collaborate or share responsibility for delivering a completed increment.

**Why it is an anti-pattern:**
It reduces teamwork, creates handover delays, and makes it difficult to deliver a potentially usable product increment.

### 3. Conclusion

The success of the Online Grocery Delivery Application depends on effective collaboration among all three Scrum accountabilities.

The Product Owner maximises product value, the Scrum Master helps the team use Scrum effectively, and the Developers create a usable product increment.

Together, they support continuous improvement and the delivery of customer-focused features.
