# Task 5 – Sprint Burndown Chart

## 1. Introduction

A Sprint Burndown Chart shows the amount of work remaining in a sprint over time. It helps the Scrum team understand whether it is progressing towards completing its sprint backlog.

The sprint duration is 10 working days, with an initial workload of 23 story points.

**Note:** The assignment refers to a supplied completed-points table, but the uploaded document does not include it. The following is an illustrative example.

## 2. Sample Sprint Burndown Data

| Sprint Day | Points Completed That Day | Remaining Story Points |
|---|---:|---:|
| Day 1 | 0 | 23 |
| Day 2 | 2 | 21 |
| Day 3 | 3 | 18 |
| Day 4 | 2 | 16 |
| Day 5 | 3 | 13 |
| Day 6 | 2 | 11 |
| Day 7 | 3 | 8 |
| Day 8 | 3 | 5 |
| Day 9 | 0 | 5 |
| Day 10 | 0 | 5 |

## 3. Burndown Calculation

Remaining Work = Previous Remaining Work − Completed Story Points

Example:

At the end of Day 2:

23 − 2 = 21 story points remaining.

At the end of Day 8:

8 − 3 = 5 story points remaining.

## 4. Interpretation of the Burndown

- The team makes steady progress from Day 2 to Day 8.
- The remaining work decreases from 23 to 5 story points.
- No additional points are completed on Days 9 and 10.
- The team finishes the sprint with 5 story points of incomplete work.
- The team should investigate the reason for the delay during the retrospective.

## 5. Conclusion

The burndown chart helps visualise the team's progress throughout the sprint. In this illustrative example, the team completes 18 out of 23 story points and carries the remaining work into the Product Backlog for future prioritisation.
