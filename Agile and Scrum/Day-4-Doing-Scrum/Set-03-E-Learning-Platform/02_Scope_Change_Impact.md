# Task 2 – Sprint Scope Change Impact Analysis

## 1. Scenario

The e-learning development team is working on a 10-day sprint. On Day 6, the Product Owner requests a new feature: Certificate Download.

The feature is required for an upcoming launch. The team must analyse its impact without compromising the Sprint Goal.

## 2. New Requirement

**Feature:** Certificate Download

**User Story:**

As a learner, I want to download my course completion certificate so that I can save and share proof of completing a course.

**Business Value:**
- Allows learners to obtain proof of course completion.
- Improves the learning experience.
- Supports the upcoming product launch.

## 3. Impact Analysis

| Area | Impact |
|---|---|
| Sprint Scope | A new feature is requested during the sprint. |
| Development Effort | Additional development and testing are required. |
| Team Capacity | The team has already committed to sprint work. |
| Testing | Certificate generation and download must be tested. |
| Sprint Goal | The team must ensure the original goal remains achievable. |
| Delivery Risk | Adding work without removing anything may cause incomplete stories. |

## 4. What Can Be Swapped Out?

The Product Owner and Developers can discuss swapping a lower-priority item of similar effort for the new certificate-download feature.

For example:

| Action | User Story | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Add | Certificate Download | Important for upcoming launch |
| Swap out | A lower-priority enhancement such as optional course recommendations | Can be delivered in a later sprint |
| Retain | Core course access and learning progress features | Necessary for the existing Sprint Goal |

This is an illustrative example. The actual story to remove depends on the team's real backlog and remaining capacity.

## 5. What Must Wait?

The following work can be considered for a future sprint if it is not essential to the current Sprint Goal:

- Optional user interface enhancements.
- Additional course recommendation features.
- Non-essential reporting improvements.
- Other lower-priority enhancements.

## 6. How the Sprint Goal Is Protected

- Discuss the request with the Developers.
- Evaluate the remaining sprint capacity.
- Avoid adding work without considering its impact.
- Swap lower-priority work where appropriate.
- Maintain the Definition of Done.
- Ensure the Sprint Goal remains achievable.

The Product Owner may negotiate the scope of the Sprint Backlog with the Developers as more is learned. If the Sprint Goal becomes obsolete, the Product Owner has the authority to cancel the sprint.

## 7. Conclusion

The certificate-download request is evaluated based on business value, effort and its impact on the existing sprint. Lower-priority work may be swapped out, while essential work remains protected. This allows the team to respond to business needs without compromising the Sprint Goal.
