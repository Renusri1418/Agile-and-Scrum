# Task 4 – Sprint Velocity Calculation

## 1. Introduction

Sprint velocity measures the number of story points completed by a Scrum team during a sprint.

Only stories that satisfy the Definition of Done are counted as completed.

## 2. Sample Completed-Points Table

The assignment refers to a completed-points table, but the uploaded document does not include the actual table. The following is an illustrative example.

| User Story | Estimated Story Points | Completion Status | Points Counted |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| US-01 – User Registration | 5 | 100% Complete | 5 |
| US-02 – Browse Courses | 8 | 100% Complete | 8 |
| US-03 – Enrol in Course | 5 | 100% Complete | 5 |
| US-04 – Course Progress | 5 | 100% Complete | 5 |
| US-05 – Certificate Download | 5 | 80% Complete | 0 |
| **Total** | **28** | | **23** |

## 3. Velocity Calculation

Velocity = Sum of Story Points Completed and Meeting the Definition of Done.

= 5 + 8 + 5 + 5

**Sprint Velocity = 23 Story Points**

## 4. How Partially Completed Work Is Counted

The Certificate Download story is estimated at 5 story points and is 80% complete.

However, it has not satisfied the Definition of Done.

Therefore:

- It is not counted as completed.
- It contributes zero points to the current sprint velocity.
- It remains incomplete in the Product Backlog.
- The Product Owner can re-prioritise it for a future sprint.

A story estimated at 5 points does not earn 4 points merely because 80% of its work is finished.

## 5. Conclusion

The team's sample sprint velocity is 23 story points. The 80%-complete certificate-download story is not counted because it has not met the Definition of Done. Velocity is based on completed stories, not partial progress.
